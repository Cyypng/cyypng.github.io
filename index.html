  • One equalizer for everything. Instead of fixing the sound in one app at a time, tapline shapes the audio for your entire computer. Music, videos, calls and games all benefit at once.

🎛️ How the Equalizer Works in Plain Language

A parametric equalizer sounds technical, but the idea is simple. Your sound is a mix of low notes (bass), middle notes (voices and instruments) and high notes (cymbals, sparkle, detail). tapline gives you a handful of adjustable handles — called bands — and each handle controls one small slice of that range.

For each band you choose three things:

  • Where in the range it sits, measured in hertz. Lower numbers are deeper sounds, higher numbers are brighter sounds.
  • How much to boost or cut that area, measured in decibels. A small nudge of a few decibels is usually plenty.
  • How wide the effect spreads, so a band can touch a tiny sliver of sound or a broad, gentle sweep.

Move a handle up and that part of the sound gets louder. Move it down and it becomes softer. That is genuinely all there is to it. You can save your adjustments as a preset and switch between presets later — one for your big headphones, one for your laptop speakers, one for late-night listening.

🎧 Correcting Your Headphones

Headphones rarely sound perfectly flat out of the box. Some are bass-heavy, some have a sharp spike in the upper range, and some sound a little dull. Enthusiasts have measured thousands of models and published correction curves for them, and tapline can read those curves directly.

  1. Open the headphone correction area inside tapline.
  2. Search for your headphone brand and model.
  3. Pick the entry that matches, or import a correction file you saved from an AutoEQ or squig.link source.
  4. Apply the correction and listen. You can fine-tune from there, or disable it with a single click at any time.

Because the correction is applied to the whole system, it works for every app on your computer at once — not just your music player.

🧑‍💻 First Run Walkthrough

Step 1: Pick your output

Choose the speakers or headphones you are listening on. tapline remembers your choice, so you only do this once per device.

Step 2: Start with a flat curve

A flat curve means "change nothing". Begin there, then add small adjustments one at a time so you can hear what each one does.

Step 3: Add an app to the mixer

Open the mixer, start playing something in the app you want to control, and it will appear in the list. Drag its slider to set its personal volume.

Step 4: Save a preset

Give your settings a name you will recognise, such as "Evening headphones" or "Work calls". You can jump back to it whenever you like.

💡 Tips for Better Results

  • Make small changes. A boost of two or three decibels is usually far more pleasant than a huge one.
  • Cut before you boost. If the sound is too harsh, gently lowering the harsh area often sounds more natural than raising everything else.
  • Test with familiar music. A song you know well makes it much easier to hear what changed.
  • Use headphone correction as your starting point, then fine-tune to taste. Personal preference always wins over a perfect graph.
  • Keep volume levels sensible. An equalizer can make quiet details audible, but it should never be used to push volume to uncomfortable levels.

🛠️ Troubleshooting

The menu bar icon does not appear

Make sure the app is actually running. Open it again from your Applications folder or wherever you saved it, and give it a few seconds to settle.

I hear no change after adjusting the equalizer

Check that the correct output device is selected, and confirm that the preset or band you edited is switched on. A flat curve or a bypassed preset will sound identical to no processing at all.

An app is missing from the mixer

The mixer fills in as apps play audio. Start playback in the app you are looking for, then return to tapline and refresh the list.

The audio stutters

Close other heavy applications for a moment, then reopen tapline. If the issue repeats, restart the app and try again.

Everything sounds too loud or distorted

Large boosts can push the sound past what the output can handle. Reduce the overall gain inside tapline or lower a couple of the boosted bands until the distortion disappears.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to install a driver?

No. tapline is built on Core Audio process taps, which are part of macOS itself. Nothing extra is added to your system.

Will it work with my headphones?

tapline does not need to know your headphones to work, but if a correction curve exists for your model, you can apply it for a noticeably more even sound.

Can I use it with my speakers too?

Yes. The equalizer applies to your selected output, whether that is headphones, built-in speakers or an external interface.

Does it affect audio from all apps?

The equalizer shapes the whole system, and the mixer lets you adjust individual apps one by one.

Can I turn it off quickly?

Yes. Each preset and correction can be switched off in a click, returning your audio to normal instantly.
